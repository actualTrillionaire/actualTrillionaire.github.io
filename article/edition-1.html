The most excellent resource I’ve found for simply explaining the Israeli election process and what everyone actually votes for can be found here:
How Do Elections in Israel Even Work? | Simply Explained

It might be worth a watch if you’re from the USA. The election process is a bit different from what we have in the US, so you may find it interesting. One of the main takeaways is that on a nation-wide basis, seats awarded in the Parliament follow popular sentiments towards the parties running candidates, rather than the candidates themselves. So, a party with 19 seats in the parliament reflect 19/120th of Israel, or 15.8%. Another thing to keep in mind is the concept of Coalitions vs Oppositions. There’s many smaller parties in Israel compared to the 2 we have in the USA, so looking at policies that require 61 seats to get enacted, it’s often not the biggest party that has the most impact, I mean they’re hardly ever a majority anyways, but the biggest coalition voting together. If a bunch of smaller opposition parties to the main one gather power together, it could be the end of that bigger party to enact policy. Negotiations are critical here, and can become incredibly nuanced.

On October 27th, 2026, elections were held to control the parliament, which we call the Knesset. At 120 seats, 61 would be considered a majority, a number that Netanyahu and his political party, Likud, would like to capture, a right-wing, nationalist party that’s established coalitions with other right and center leaning parties to establish a control of the Knesset. Naturally, they oppose Palestinian statehood and support Jewish expansion of settlements in the West Bank, officially opposing a Palestinian state (source: https://israelpolicyforum.org/likud/).
