The most excellent resource I’ve found for simply explaining the Israeli election process and what everyone actually votes for can be found here, better than the above articles:
How Do Elections in Israel Even Work? | Simply Explained

It might be worth a watch if you’re from the USA. The election process is a bit different from what we have in the US, so you may find it interesting. One of the main takeaways is that on a nation-wide basis, seats awarded in the Parliament follow a system of voting towards the parties, rather than the candidates themselves. So, a party with 19 seats in the parliament reflect 19/120 seats, or 15.8% of the Knesset, as is the case with the Arab-led parties who won seats recently. Another thing to keep in mind is the concept of Coalitions vs Oppositions. There’s many smaller parties in Israel compared to the 2 we have in the USA, so looking at policies that require 61 seats to get enacted, it’s often not the biggest party that has the most impact, which are hardly ever a majority anyways, but the biggest coalition voting together. If a bunch of smaller opposition parties to the alternatives gather power together, it could be the end of the opposition to enact policy, where a new government would be created. Negotiations are critical here, and can become incredibly nuanced.

On October 27th, 2026, elections were held to control the parliament. At 120 seats, 61 would be considered a majority, a number that Netanyahu and his political party, Likud, would like to have captured. Likud is a right-wing, nationalist party that’s established coalitions with other right and center leaning parties to try to establish control of the Knesset. Naturally, they oppose Palestinian statehood and support Jewish expansion of settlements in the West Bank, officially opposing a Palestinian state (source: https://israelpolicyforum.org/likud/). Likud is the largest party in the Israeli Parliament.
