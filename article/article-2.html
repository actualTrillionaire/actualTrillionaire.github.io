Midterm 2 Minute News Roundup

A blue tsunami has hit the country.

Democrat Gina Hinojosa won the Texas governor’s race, making her the first Latina governor of Texas, defeating Republican Greg Abbot. The newly drawn congressional map Republicans drew in preparation for the election saw no gains and a loss of one seat. The plan backfired. Texas is now blue for the first time in 40 years.

Women in this election have made historic victories. Democrat Amy Klobuchar was elected the first woman governor of Minnesota, and Democrat Amy Acton was elected the first woman governor of Ohio, defeating Republican Vivek Ramaswamy. New Mexico now has the first Native American governor, Deb Haaland. Peggy Flanagan, a senator in Minnesota, also becomes the first Native American to be elected to the Senate.

Georgia now has the first Black woman governor, Keisha Lance Bottoms, defeating billionaire healthcare executive Republican Rick Jackson. Other historic firsts for Black governors include wins in Florida (Republican Byron Donalds), Nevada (Democrat Aaron Ford), and Wisconsin (Democrat David Crowley).

Democrat Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan has become the first Muslim elected to the US senate.

Trans and nonbinary candidates also take big wins:

33 year-old Non-binary Japanese-American Democratic Socialist Bentley Hudgins wins in Georgia, overcoming threats of homelessness, poor access to healthcare, and a disability making her legally deaf at age 19, requiring surgery, a testament to resiliency.

Joanna Whaley, a trans woman and former megachurch pastor who has survived 15 years of conversion therapy, won her House seat in Michigan, becoming the first transwoman in the legislature.

Allison Montgomery, a trans woman, also made history being elected to the House in Alabama, along with Dina Nina Martinez-Rutherford making history for the House in Wisconsin, and Quinnie Perez making history for the House in Florida.

Jaelynn Scott, DSA, has become the first black trans legislature in the country in Washington with a landslide 88% of the vote.

Florida’s Democratic Socialist candidate Angie Nixon loses by just 2.9% of the vote in a string of growing support for the Democratic Socialists of America (DSA). In New York, with endorsements from mayor Zohran Mamdani, Darializa Avila Chevalier and Claire Valdez won congressional seats, and with support from the DSA, Donovan McKinney in Michigan and Chris Rabb in Pennsylvania took seats as well. A major win goes to Melat Kiros, a Democratic Socialist in Colorado who became the first Black woman elected to congress in Colorado’s history.

Elon Musk, after spending nearly $500,000,000 on elections for the Republicans, seemingly wasted this money on nothing. Musk, whose Tesla, X, and SpaceX corporations have bases in Texas, are now led by a Democratic governor after Abbot got ousted, one much more critical of him.
